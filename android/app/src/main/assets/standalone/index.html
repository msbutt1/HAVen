How it works

What the ring on the Today screen means, in plain words.

Why it matters

Using shaking tools like drills, grinders and breakers for a long time, day after day, can damage your hands and arms. It can cause tingling, numb fingers, fingers that go white in the cold, and a weaker grip. It builds up slowly and can be permanent.

Points

The sleeve feels how hard the tool shakes your arm. Every minute you use a tool adds points. A tool that shakes harder adds points much faster.

Only real tool time counts. Walking, waving or shaking hands adds nothing, and neither does holding a tool without gripping it.

The two levels

Under 100 points: low.
100 points: action level. From here your employer has to act to cut your vibration, for example other tasks, other tools or more breaks.
400 points: daily limit. Stop using vibrating tools for the rest of the day.

These are the levels in the UK and EU rules for hand-arm vibration. Your supervisor may set a lower limit for you.

Tool time

The phone knows when you are using a power tool and counts your tool time. While a tool runs, it tells you how long until you reach each level. After 20 minutes without a break, it reminds you to take one.

Simple tips

  • Take short breaks from the tool.
  • Let the tool do the work. Do not grip harder than you need to.
  • Keep your hands warm and dry.
  • Tell your supervisor if your fingers tingle, go numb or turn white.

Good to know

Each day is saved by itself and starts again at midnight. Set up the sleeve each time you put it on.

This is an estimate from a sensor on your wrist, not an official measurement, and staying under the action level does not make vibration safe. VibeGuard is a test version, not a medical device.
