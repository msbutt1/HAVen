What the rules suggest

Based on EU Directive 2002/44/EC and UK HSE guidance, with the daily exposure worked out the ISO 5349-1 way. An estimate from a sensor on your wrist, not an official measurement.

Day by day

How it works

The ring on your Today screen shows how much tool vibration your hands and arms have taken today.

Why it matters

Shaking tools like drills, grinders and breakers can damage your hands and arms when you use them a lot, day after day. Fingers tingle, go numb or turn white in the cold, and your grip gets weaker. It builds up slowly and can be permanent.

How points add up

The sleeve feels how hard your tool shakes your arm. Every minute with a tool in your hands adds points.

Gentle toolpoints add up slowly
Hard-shaking toolpoints add up fast

Only real tool time counts. Walking, waving or shaking hands adds nothing.

The two levels

0100400
Low

Under 100 points.

Action level, 100 points

Your employer has to cut your vibration: other tasks, other tools or more breaks.

Daily limit, 400 points

No more vibrating tools for the rest of the day.

These are the levels in the UK and EU rules. Your supervisor may set a lower limit for you.

An example

A grinder that shakes your arm hard adds about 72 points an hour. You reach the action level after about 1 hour 23 minutes, and the daily limit after about 5 and a half hours. A gentler tool takes much longer.

Two tools in one day add up. So does the same tool used again after a break.

Tool time

The phone knows when you are using a power tool. While it runs, the Today screen tells you how long until the next level. When you reach the action level or the daily limit, the phone sounds an alarm, even when the app is in the background.

Simple tips

  1. Take short breaks from the tool.
  2. Let the tool do the work. Don't grip harder than you need to.
  3. Keep your hands warm and dry.
  4. Tell your supervisor if your fingers tingle, go numb or turn white.

Over months and years

The Overview shows your average over the week, the month or the year. The daily limits apply to each day on its own: days are never added up. Experts estimate how many years of days like that it takes before about 1 in 10 workers get white, numb fingers. The higher your daily points, the sooner. It is a guide for groups of workers, not a test of your own hands.

Good to know

Each day is saved on its own and starts again at midnight. Set up the sleeve each time you put it on.

This is an estimate from a sensor on your wrist, not an official measurement: official checks measure at the hand. Staying under the action level does not make vibration safe. HAVen is a test version, not a medical device.

Based on: EU Directive 2002/44/EC; UK Control of Vibration at Work Regulations 2005 and HSE's points system; ISO 5349-1 (daily exposure and Annex C); ISO/TS 22270 (wrist-worn monitoring).

For short demos with the massage gun. The vibration reading and the formula are unchanged; only the clock runs faster for points and tool time. The Today screen says so while it is on.

Count only while gripping

The exposure formula's grip rule (exposure.py grip_gate). On, a light hold on the tool may not count. Off suits the massage-gun demo. Changing it only affects points from now on.

Demo history (Month and Year)

Reset demo clears today's points and refills the 12 months before today with made-up days, so the Overview's Week, Month and Year have a story to tell: higher at first, lower after rotation and lower-vibration tools. Sample days are marked on the Overview and never replace a real day.

Overview sample week

Tool calibration (IMU)

Readings

Daily exposure estimate (signals exposure.py): A(8) = √(Σ a_hv² Δt / 8 h), points = 2 · a_hv² · hours = 100 · (A(8)/2.5)². Counted only while the tool detector says tool; with "Count only while gripping" on, also only while the wearer is not known to be relaxed (grip < 0.10 of the setup squeeze). EU 2002/44/EC and UK CVWR 2005: action value 2.5 m/s² (100 points), limit value 5.0 m/s² (400 points). Wrist-worn, unweighted IMU estimate (monitoring, not ISO 5349-2).
